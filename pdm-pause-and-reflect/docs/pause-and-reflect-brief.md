# Pause & Reflect: Discussion Starter

**Flood Recovery Project: agri-input and cash distribution, Eastmere and Northvale**
*Source: Post-Distribution Monitoring, November 2025. 322 of 605 households (53%).*

## Headline

Satisfaction is near-universal: all 322 respondents rated input quality "good", 99% found inputs appropriate to their context, 94% found them sufficient, and none paid a fee to receive support. Yet only 78% had planted seeds. The gap sits in one place.

## 1. Package 2 is not yet delivering

- 18% of Package 2 households (16 of 87) have planted, against 100% for Packages 1 and 3.
- 30 respondents said they did not receive seeds; others gave no reason. The survey does not record what each package contained.
- Package 2 also has the lowest sufficiency (86%), the longest waits (59% over one hour) and all eight reports that the cash differed from the amount announced.

**Action:** reconcile procurement and distribution records against Package 2's intended contents before the next round.

## 2. Training is the main unmet need

- 87% said they needed training or guidance; for Package 3 it was 99%.
- Need is high in every age group (81–92%), so targeting by age adds little. Blanket coverage is simpler.

**Action:** schedule demonstrations and follow-up visits alongside distribution.

## 3. Access costs differ by location

- Eastmere: 50% waited over an hour at the site; 10% had difficulty accessing cash, mostly citing poor roads.
- Northvale: 31% travelled over an hour, and average transport cost was LCU 264 (Eastmere: LCU 98) from a LCU 2,700 grant.

**Action:** stagger time slots in Eastmere; test closer distribution points or transport support in Northvale.

## 4. District gaps are really package gaps

Package 1 went almost entirely to Northvale; Packages 2 and 3 went to Eastmere. Place and package effects cannot be separated. Document allocation rules, or randomise, so the next round supports fair comparison.

## 5. Basic needs remain unmet in Eastmere

Only 28% of Eastmere households meet all or most basic needs, versus 99% in Northvale. Check whether cash alone is adequate and whether targeting reached the most vulnerable.

## Caution

Uniformly positive answers (100% on quality; 100% of those who answered on fairness) may reflect courtesy bias or coarse response scales. Treat them as reassurance, not proof, and add outcome measures at harvest.

## Questions for the session

1. What did Package 2 contain, and what happened to the seeds?
2. Who delivers training, and when?
3. Can distribution points move closer to Northvale households?
4. What should a harvest-stage follow-up measure?
