# PDM survey instrument: structure overview

The Post-Distribution Monitoring tool for the agricultural input and cash distribution has 12 sections (A to L) and takes about 30 minutes. The wording below is summarised, not reproduced.

| Section | Purpose |
|---|---|
| A. Consent | Voluntary participation, confidentiality, right to stop |
| B. Background | Date, district, village, enumerator details |
| C. Household | Respondent sex, relationship to household head, age |
| D. Selection and targeting | Was the selection explained, which criteria, was it fair |
| E. Receipt of input | Fees or favours, sharing of inputs, cash amount, package, quality |
| F. Timeliness and transport | Travel time, waiting time, transport cost, cash access |
| G. Feedback and response | Awareness and use of feedback channels, follow-up |
| H. Protection | Safety in transit and at the site, risk of harm |
| I. Use of cash | What cash was spent on, ability to meet basic needs |
| J. Use of agri-input | Whether inputs were used, seeds planted, fertiliser applied, resale or sharing |
| K. Effectiveness and suitability | Appropriateness, training need, sufficiency |
| L. Closing | Open feedback, GPS, end time |

## Design observations

- **Needed versus received training.** K2 asks whether training was needed, not whether it was received.
- **Package contents are not captured**, which limits diagnosis of problems such as non-planting.
- **Reasons for non-use** (J1a, J3a) are free text and were not completed by most of the people who qualified to answer them.
- **Ceiling effects.** Quality and fairness items produced almost no variation.
- **Skip logic is sound** and aligns with the respondent flow, which kept the interview short.
